\section{Spacetime local stochastic Pauli noise}\label{sec:correlated}

This section extends the finite-torus memory theorem. The correction
rule, correction-clock marginal and decoder are unchanged. The noise
is now specified by a classical joint trajectory law rather than,
in general, by an additive Lindblad noise generator.

\subsection{Lindbladian correction, trajectory-defined noise}

Fix integers \(g\ge1\) and \(r_0\ge0\), independently of \(L\).
There are at most \(g\) anchor labels at each spatial lattice
vertex. Each anchor \(\alpha\) has a fixed footprint of data qubits
within sup radius \(r_0\) of its position. Enlarge this fixed radius
if necessary to contain the vertices of both the associated primal
and dual faces. The time unit is the inverse correction rate, as
in Section~\ref{sec:model}. Elementary locations are
\[
 (\alpha,j),\qquad \mathsf I_j=(j,j+1],\qquad j=0,1,\ldots .
\]
Let \(\mathcal F\) be a random set of such locations. For a strength
\(0<p<1\), impose the spacetime local-stochastic condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-stochastic}
 \Prb\{S\subseteq\mathcal F\}\le p^{|S|}
 \quad\text{for every deterministic finite set }S
             \text{ of distinct elementary locations}.
\end{equation}
An error at a time in \(\mathsf I_j\) is a Pauli string whose entire support
is contained in the union of footprints of the faulty locations
\((\alpha,j)\in\mathcal F\). Its labels, times and multiplicities
may otherwise be arbitrary, subject to local finiteness on bounded
time intervals. A single faulty location may apply several nearby
single-qubit factors at once, paying for that location once.
Even an extensive simultaneous string is allowed when it has the
required cover by faulty locations in its own time cell.

The two correction-clock families retain their joint product law
of independent rate-one Poisson processes. Noise may correlate
across positions, dates, Pauli types and sectors, and with the
correction clocks; only this correction-clock \emph{marginal} is
fixed. The complete classical noise law must be independent of
the hidden logical input and its reference. Adaptive choices based
on exogenous randomness, the syndrome and previous Pauli-and-clock
history are allowed when their joint fault set obeys
\eqref{eq:local-stochastic}. No extra measurement of the logical
subsystem is part of a noise history.

A definite order at ties between faults and correction attempts is
part of the history. For proofs, factor each simultaneous Pauli
string into its nonidentity single-qubit factors in a fixed internal
order, inserting no correction attempt between them. All these
virtual stages have the original time and fault cover. Readout at
a deterministic time is after all events at that time. The encoded
state at zero precedes every allowed error. These conventions allow
noise pulses at deterministic time-cell boundaries.

We call this model \emph{Lindbladian correction, trajectory-defined
noise}. Each correction attempt is the Poisson unravelling of
\(\sum_p(\Diss[Z_p\Pi_p^X]+\Diss[X_p\Pi_p^Z])\) on a
syndrome-definite branch. A general correlated history need not
yield a Markov noise process or a semigroup for the combined
evolution. Its ensemble is defined directly by averaging its
Pauli trajectories on encoded inputs. The input-independence
hypothesis is essential to that channel interpretation.

\begin{lemma}[Dependence on the time grid]\label{lem:grid}
For any law satisfying \eqref{eq:local-stochastic} on unit cells
with parameter \(p\), a unit grid shifted by any fixed amount
admits a covering fault set with local-stochastic parameter at
most \(2\sqrt p\). Aligned subdivision of each unit cell into
\(k\) equal cells, for any integer \(k\ge1\), admits parameter at
most \(p^{1/k}\). The spatial footprints can be kept unchanged.
Bounds exceeding one are understood as vacuous.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On the shifted grid declare a location faulty if an old faulty
cell with the same anchor intersects its interval. At time zero,
intersect intervals with the nonnegative time axis; old cells
before zero can be treated as empty. Each new interval intersects
at most two old intervals, and each old interval intersects at
most two new ones, using the same half-open endpoint convention.
For a prescribed set of \(m\) new locations, expand at most \(2^m\)
choices of an old covering key per location. Every such choice
contains at least \(m/2\) distinct old keys. Its probability is
at most \(p^{m/2}\) by \eqref{eq:local-stochastic}. A union bound
gives \(2^mp^{m/2}=(2\sqrt p)^m\).
The support cover is preserved because the old cell containing an
error time intersects the new cell containing that time.

For aligned refinement, declare all \(k\) children of a faulty
old location faulty. A prescribed set of \(m\) refined locations
has at least \(m/k\) distinct old parents, so its joint probability
is at most \(p^{m/k}\). The same footprints cover every original
error. These are changes of the strength parameter, not merely
a fixed rescaling of constants.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exponential lifetime under spacetime local stochastic
Pauli noise]\label{thm:correlated}
For every fixed \(g,r_0\) there exist \(p_*,c,C>0\) and an integer
\(L_0\), independent of the admissible joint history law and of
the logical input, such that the following holds. For every integer
\(L\ge L_0\), every history law with the correction-clock marginal,
bounded footprints, local finiteness and input-independence
specified above, and strength \(p\le p_*\) in
\eqref{eq:local-stochastic} on unit time cells, the current-syndrome
decoder of Section~\ref{sec:decoder}, from an encoded start, obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:correlated-memory}
 \inf_{0\le t\le e^{cL}}\
 \min_{\|\psi\|=1}
 \langle\psi|(\readout\circ\mathsf{Store}_t\circ\enc)
       (|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)|\psi\rangle
       \ge1-Ce^{-cL}.
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathsf{Store}_t\) is the trajectory-defined storage ensemble.
The same bound holds for overlap with any pure input and untouched
reference, uniformly in the designated logical qubit and fixed state
of the other five. It allows bounded-range multi-qubit and adaptive
faults satisfying the stated joint condition, and concerns
stochastic Pauli noise only.
\end{theorem}

The proof occupies the remaining subsections. All estimates for
correlated faults are unconditional at deterministic dates; a
conditional bound after an arbitrary revealed past is not assumed.

\subsection{Noise models included in the hypothesis}

Independent single-qubit Pauli Poisson jumps of deterministic rates
\(\gamma_{p,P}(t)\le\eta\) satisfy
\eqref{eq:local-stochastic} with \(p\le1-e^{-3\eta}\le3\eta\):
use one anchor per qubit and mark its unit cell when any noise
jump occurs. Distinct qubit-time cells are independent by the
product Poisson law. Thus the extension includes the constant-rate
model of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, and its uniformly bounded
deterministic time-dependent version. Fresh product Pauli layers
with each one-qubit nonidentity probability at most \(3\eta\),
with at most \(n_l\) prescribed layers per unit interval, likewise
give \(p\le3n_l\eta\) by a cell union bound and independence between
distinct cells. Here \(n_l\) is fixed independently of \(L\).

Independent bounded-range multi-qubit Pauli jumps are another
special case. Assign each jump type to a fixed anchor in its
support, with its support inside that anchor's footprint.
Suppose the sum of deterministic intensities at each anchor is
at most \(\lambda\). The event that any such jump occurs in a
unit anchor cell has probability at most \(1-e^{-\lambda}\);
these events are independent across anchors and unit intervals.
This proves \eqref{eq:local-stochastic} with
\(p\le1-e^{-\lambda}\). A bound \(\lambda\) on the sum of jump
rates touching each qubit also suffices when qubits are the
anchors, since the types assigned to a qubit are a subset of
the types touching it. For independent constant-rate jumps added
to the correction, the usual generator is the correction
Lindbladian plus \(\sum_\tau\lambda_\tau\Diss[P_\tau]\).
The Pauli-branch calculation in Section~\ref{sec:quantum} proves
this realization directly on encoded inputs. The general
correlated model makes no such sum-generator assertion.

\subsection{Geometry and a joint coarse-bin cost}

Keep \(R=2000100\). Take an integer
\(B\ge\max\{10,r_0+10\}\), and use \(M,b,T,m_f,Q_z,U_z\) from
\eqref{eq:block-parameters}, with \(T=1000B\) an integer. We
require \(L\) sufficiently large for the radius-\((R+6)\) block
neighbourhoods to lift injectively. In the dual sector use its
fixed half-cell coordinate translation.

Let \(\mathcal C(z,k)\) be failure of either of the two
clock-only tests in Definition~\ref{def:bad}: the exclusion of
a chronological chain of \(m_f\) attempts and completion of
every target clearance schedule by \(T\). Its probability is
bounded using only the correction-clock marginal by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clock-only}
 \pi_B:=CB^4(96e/1000)^{1000T}
 +(2B+1)^4(4\cdot2^{12})^{8B+2}e^{-T/2}
 \ \longrightarrow\ 0.
\end{equation}
This is exactly the clock part of the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:bad-prob}: ordered repeated labels use Poisson
factorial moments, and the schedule uses distinct-vertex
exponential waits. Correlation with faults does not alter
this marginal estimate.

Let \(\mathcal V(z,k)\) be the deterministic set of elementary
anchor-time cells in slab \(I_k\) whose possible footprints can
include a face incident on \(U_z\). Use the full allowed
radius-\(r_0\) collar, rather than the realized fault support.
Define the new bad-bin indicator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:correlated-bad}
 J_{\rm ls}(z,k)=
 \mathbf1\{\mathcal C(z,k)\ \text{or}\
                      \mathcal F\cap\mathcal V(z,k)\ne\varnothing\}.
\end{equation}
The elementary time cells partition the slab because \(T\) is
integer. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope-size}
 H_0=R+6,\qquad D_0=(4H_0+1)^4,\qquad
 V_B=gT\left\lceil2B(2R+1)+2r_0+12\right\rceil^4.
\end{equation}
All clock inputs and spatial anchors of \(\mathcal V(z,k)\)
are within \(H_0\) coarse blocks of \(z\), and
\(|\mathcal V(z,k)|\le V_B\). Indeed \(U_z\) has coordinate
width at most \(2B(2R+1)\); the footprint collar and face-vertex
allowance add \(2r_0+12\), including the fixed dual translation.
Since \(B\ge r_0+10\), these physical collars fit in the stated
fixed coarse margin. A larger fixed cell-coordinate convention
can be absorbed by increasing that margin and \(H_0\) once.

If \(J_{\rm ls}(z,k)=0\), there is no noise face incident on
\(U_z\), even at an intermediate factor of an atomic Pauli.
The backward discrepancy argument in
Lemma~\ref{lem:predecessor} consequently gives agreement with
the clean cut throughout the slab. A discrepancy from its
boundary would need a chronological chain from distance at
least \(RB-2>m_f+5\), contradicting the clock test.
The coordinate confinement and clearance proofs then give all
five predecessor implications of that lemma for every even
starting syndrome. They also give its whole-slab visit
implication, now including the virtual intermediate states.
A distant atomic string changes no edge in \(U_z\) when the
test is good. At a slab boundary the starting state for the
next slab is after all marks of the preceding slab, so
deterministic fault-time pulses cause no exception.

\begin{lemma}[Joint bad-bin bound]\label{lem:joint-bins}
For every \(b_*\in(0,1)\), one can choose \(B\) as above and then
\(p_*>0\), depending only on \(g,r_0,b_*\), so that for every
admissible law at \(p\le p_*\) and every deterministic finite
set \(\mathcal U\) of distinct coarse spacetime bins,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-bins}
 \Prb\{J_{\rm ls}(u)=1\text{ for all }u\in\mathcal U\}
       \le b_*^{|\mathcal U|}.
\end{equation}
This assertion is uniform over the dates in \(\mathcal U\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(m=|\mathcal U|\), with the empty case immediate.
Join bins with the same slab index and coarse spatial distance
at most \(2H_0\). A greedy selection retains
\(\ell\ge m/D_0\) nonadjacent bins. Their potential elementary
fault sets \(\mathcal V\) are disjoint, and their clock-only
tests use disjoint Poisson increments. The latter tests are
independent under the product clock marginal, regardless of
their correlations with the noise.

Partition these \(\ell\) bins into a specified set
\(\mathcal A\) requiring clock failure and a set \(\mathcal B\)
requiring a fault-envelope hit. The clock intersection has
probability at most \(\pi_B^{|\mathcal A|}\).
For the other intersection choose one elementary key from each
\(\mathcal V\). Those keys are distinct because the envelopes
are disjoint. Equation~\eqref{eq:local-stochastic} and a union
bound over at most \(V_B^{|\mathcal B|}\) choices give an upper
bound \((V_Bp)^{|\mathcal B|}\).
Cauchy--Schwarz for the two event indicators bounds their
intersection by
\(\pi_B^{|\mathcal A|/2}(V_Bp)^{|\mathcal B|/2}\), without
conditioning either marginal on the other.

Sum over the partitions. Every selected bin is bad only if it
has at least one of these two causes. Therefore the probability
that all original bins are bad is at most
\[
 \bigl(\sqrt{\pi_B}+\sqrt{V_Bp}\bigr)^\ell.
\]
Choose \(B\) so large that
\(\sqrt{\pi_B}\le b_*^{D_0}/2\), using
\eqref{eq:clock-only}, and then \(p_*>0\) so small that
\(\sqrt{V_Bp_*}\le b_*^{D_0}/2\).
The displayed expression is at most
\(b_*^{D_0\ell}\le b_*^m\). Only the unconditional fault
inclusion bound and the clock marginal were used.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Joint costs for localized witness families}

Use \eqref{eq:comparison} and \eqref{eq:proxy} with
\(J_{\rm ls}\) in place of \(\Bad\), writing the resulting
fields as \(H_{\rm ls}^a\) and \(P_{\rm ls}^a\).
All-one initialization at the deterministic date \(a\) is
unchanged. The deterministic implications just proved give,
from the encoded start,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ls-domination}
 \begin{split}
 Q_z\text{ occupied at }kT&\ \Longrightarrow\
                               H_{\rm ls}^0(z,k)=0,\\
 Q_z\text{ visited in slab }k&\ \Longrightarrow\
                               P_{\rm ls}^0(z,k).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The visits in the second line include every single-face
intermediate stage of each simultaneous Pauli string.
The induction is the one in \eqref{eq:domination}: endpoint
occupation in a good bin has a zero predecessor in every
\(A_s\), and any visit has at least one in \(\Delta\).

\begin{proposition}[Joint localized-witness cost]\label{prop:joint-witness}
For every \(A>0\), the parameters can be chosen so that the following
holds in every deterministic window of depth \(w\) after a date
\(a\), with \(2Rw<M/2\). At each site \(u\) of that window
there are certificate events \(\mathcal E_{\rm ls}^a(u,r)\),
with allowed radii
\[
 r=K_0(n+1),\qquad K_0=10(R+6),\qquad n=0,1,\ldots ,
\]
such that \(P_{\rm ls}^a(u)\) implies at least one event.
For every finite predetermined family of mutually disjoint closed
\(\rho\)-balls \(\Ball_\rho(u_j,r_j)\), with centers in that window
and allowed radii, their joint cost satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-witness}
 \Prb\left(\bigcap_j\mathcal E_{\rm ls}^a(u_j,r_j)\right)
      \le \exp\left(-A\sum_jr_j\right).
\end{equation}
The events are unions of requirements on bad sink bins localized
inside their named balls. No independence between the events is
asserted.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A bad bin at \(u\) gives a one-sink candidate with \(n=0\).
Otherwise \(P_{\rm ls}^a(u)\) supplies a zero predecessor
\((y,k-1)\) within spatial distance \(R\).
There is no zero at the all-one bottom date. At a later date
lift that predecessor's backward cone to the integer lattice,
copy its maps, and put constant-one maps at the bottom and
everywhere outside the cone. The recursion is finite and
time-directed, so its root value is the unique one from its
bottom data and hence the maximal trajectory value.

This graph satisfies all the hypotheses of the deterministic
SST Theorem 22 checked in Section~\ref{sec:contours}: each
nonconstant map has finite nonempty type-\(s\) predecessor
set \(A_s\), constants have no outgoing dependencies, and
each dependence edge decreases time by one. That theorem
supplies a connected contour at a zero. SST Definitions 17
and 21 and Eq.~(2.16) make its sinks distinct bad sites and
exclude constant-one cutoff sites \cite{SST}.
At each date the cone width is at most \(2Rw<M/2\), so
distinct lifted sinks at that date have distinct physical
bin indices. Distinct dates already give distinct indices.
This argument is deterministic and has no fault-independence
hypothesis.

Let \(n\) be the number of edges per charge and \(m\) the
number of sinks. The linear forms \eqref{eq:polar} have
increments at least \(0,0,0,0,1\) on the five prescribed
types, sum to zero, and have respective losses at most
\(R,R,R,R,4R\) on arbitrary offsets in \(\Delta\).
Consequently the flux calculation
\eqref{eq:edges-sinks}, using SST Lemmas 38 and 40, gives
\(m\ge1+n/\alpha\), where \(\alpha=1+8R\).
The enumeration \eqref{eq:contour-count} uses SST
arXiv:2202.10999v3, proof Eq.~(5.28); its weaker Lemma 39
statement Eq.~(5.15) would also suffice.
After the bounded predecessor choice and the direct case,
the number of candidates at \(u\) is at most \(C_0C_1^n\),
with constants depending only on \(R\).

Define \(\mathcal E_{\rm ls}^a(u,K_0(n+1))\) by taking the
union over those deterministic cone candidates and requiring
only that each named sink is bad. A connected contour has
\(5n\) edges. Hence every sink is within \(5Rn+R\) spatial
units and \(5n+1\) dates of \(u\), and lies in its named
ball. Even the local clock/fault inputs of its bad test
fit after adding the \(H_0\) collar. Empty events can be
used at other radii. The contour implication gives the
claimed cover.

For the prescribed disjoint balls, expand the intersection
of the candidate unions into tuples of deterministic
contours. Within one candidate the sinks are distinct by
the embedding and lift argument. Across candidates in a
tuple they are distinct because the balls are disjoint
as sets on the actual torus times time. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:joint-bins} once to the \emph{union} of all
sink bins in that tuple. With \(n_j=r_j/K_0-1\), summing
over the tuples gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tuple-charge}
 \Prb\left(\bigcap_j\mathcal E_{\rm ls}^a(u_j,r_j)\right)
 \le\prod_j\left[C_0C_1^{n_j}
                       b_*^{\,1+n_j/\alpha}\right].
\end{equation}
The displayed product is a counting bound: the number of
choices multiplies and the number of distinct sinks adds.
It does not factor the underlying noise law.

Choose \(b_*>0\) sufficiently small so that
\(C_0C_1^n b_*^{1+n/\alpha}\le
e^{-AK_0(n+1)}\) for every \(n\ge0\), by bounding the constant
term and the coefficient of \(n\) in its logarithm.
Then choose \(B,p_*\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:joint-bins}.
This proves \eqref{eq:joint-witness}.
Balls of radius above \(M/2\) may wrap spatially around
the torus. The sink sets are physical indices without
repeated copies, the quotient volume bound remains valid,
and only disjoint actual balls are used. No independence
claim is needed for wrapping or nonwrapping balls.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Centered crossing under correlated faults]
\label{prop:ls-path}
For sufficiently large fixed \(A\), and the resulting choices of
parameters, there are \(C,\kappa>0\) such that for every
deterministic window above, every specified anchor \(u\) in it,
and every real \(s\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ls-path}
 \Prb\left\{\begin{array}{l}
 \text{a finite path in }P_{\rm ls}^a
       \text{ starts at }u,\text{ stays in the window,}\\
 \text{and reaches }\rho\text{-distance at least }s
 \end{array}\right\}\le Ce^{-\kappa s}.
\end{equation}
Path steps have \(\rho\)-distance at most \(R\), and the
constants are uniform over dates, \(L\), and admissible laws.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a realizing finite path, assign an occurring certificate
ball to each distinct site. Greedily select disjoint balls
in decreasing radius. A discarded ball meets a selected
one with at least its radius, so the selected triple
enlargements cover the path. The covers of its endpoints
are connected by a simple chain of selected centers whose
successive distances are at most \(3r+3r'+R\).
If \(v\) is the sum of radii in this chain and \(j\) its
length, endpoint displacements and the triangle inequality
give \(s\le6v+R(j-1)\le7v\), since every radius exceeds \(R\).
This is a product-metric argument, including on the torus.

For fixed ordered radii, the initial center has at most
\((6r_1+1)^5\) choices around the specified anchor, and
subsequent centers have at most
\((6r_i+6r_{i+1}+2R+1)^5\) choices. As in the explicit
entropy calculation in Proposition~\ref{prop:path}, their
product is at most \(e^{60v}\).
There are at most \(2^v\) ordered compositions of \(v\).
For each deterministic chain with disjoint original balls,
use the joint estimate \eqref{eq:joint-witness} to pay
\(e^{-Av}\) for all its certificates at once.
Thus, taking \(A>100\),
\[
 \Prb(\text{crossing})\le
 \sum_{v\ge\max\{1,\lceil s/7\rceil\}}
        e^{-(A-60-\log2)v}
 \le Ce^{-\kappa s}.
\]
Adaptive selection after observing the path causes no
probability factorization: the union bound enumerates
deterministic chains before applying their joint cost.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Unconditional overlapping windows}

\begin{proposition}[Correlated cumulative history bound]
\label{prop:ls-history}
For each named CSS sector there exist \(C_0,\beta>0\) and
\(L_0\), uniform over the admissible history laws at
\(p\le p_*\), such that from an encoded start, for every
\(L\ge L_0\) and deterministic \(t\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ls-history}
 \Prb\left\{\begin{array}{l}
 \text{a sector syndrome component has diameter }\ge L/100\\
 \text{by time }t,\text{ including virtual single-face stages}
 \end{array}\right\}
 \le\min\{1,C_0(1+t)e^{-\beta L}\}.
\end{equation}
Diameter is ambient torus sup diameter.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set \(h=\lfloor M/[100(R+6)]\rfloor\) and \(a_j=jh\).
For all sufficiently large \(M\), \(h\ge1\) and a
two-epoch window of depth \(2h\) satisfies \(4Rh<M/2\).
On the original joint marks define
\(H_j=H_{\rm ls}^{a_j}\), all ones at its own deterministic
start. Define \(G_j\) as equality of \(H_j,H_{j+1}\)
everywhere at \(a_{j+2}\), and \(V_j\) as absence of a
spatially connected set of diameter at least \(M/200\)
in \(P_{\rm ls}^{a_j}(\cdot,k)\) at any of the \(2h\)
window dates.

During the overlap \(H_j\le H_{j+1}\), by all-one
initialization of the latter and monotonicity of each
common update. A disagreeing endpoint has a disagreeing
predecessor unless its map is constant, which cannot
transmit disagreement. Trace one such predecessor per
date down to \(a_{j+1}\). The resulting path consists
of zeros of the older comparison, hence sites in
\(P_{\rm ls}^{a_j}\); it has temporal span \(h\) and
steps at most \(R\). Proposition~\ref{prop:ls-path}
and a union over final sites give
\[
 \Prb(G_j^c)\le CM^4e^{-\kappa h}.
\]
If \(V_j\) fails, a spatial path in one of those dates
reaches distance \(M/200\) from some initial block.
Summing the same path bound over dates and anchors gives
\[
 \Prb(V_j^c)\le 2ChM^4e^{-\kappa M/200}.
\]
These are unconditional bounds at each deterministic
\(j\). The joint inclusion bound applies to any specified
dates, so no fresh conditional noise law at \(a_j\) is
needed.

On \(\bigcap_{0\le j<n}(G_j\cap V_j)\), the same
deterministic comparison iteration as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:iteration} applies. Explicitly,
\(H_{\rm ls}^0=H_{i-1}\) at \(a_i\) for \(i=1\) by
identity. Equality propagates through epoch \(i\)
under common maps. Then \(G_{i-1}\) equates
\(H_{i-1}\) with \(H_i\) at \(a_{i+1}\), proving
the assertion inductively. The visit proxy uses
the preceding boundary bits and the current bad bin.
Thus its values agree as well on every needed slab,
including the earliest predecessor date of each epoch.
Use \(V_0\) in epoch zero and \(V_{i-1}\) in epoch \(i\ge1\),
together with \eqref{eq:ls-domination}.

A physical or virtual component of diameter at least
\(L/100\) maps to a connected set of visited blocks
of diameter at least \(M/100-1\ge M/200\), by the
physical/coarse inequality \(b(d+1)\) proved in
Lemma~\ref{lem:iteration}. This contradicts the
applicable \(V_j\). The visit implication includes
each intermediate factor at its original time and
the final mark at a slab boundary, with the next slab
initialized after all those marks.

For \(t>0\), take \(n=\lceil t/(hT)\rceil\).
The union bound over these failures is at most
\[
 CnM^4\left(e^{-\kappa h}
                    +2h e^{-\kappa M/200}\right).
\]
Some window tests extend beyond \(t\), which only
enlarges the bounding event. With \(B,T,R\) fixed,
\(M\) and \(h\) grow linearly in \(L\);
\(n\le1+t\). Absorb the remaining polynomial factors
in a smaller positive exponential rate, as in the
proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:history}, to obtain
\eqref{eq:ls-history}. At zero the syndrome is empty.
There is no conditioning on earlier successful
overlaps and no independence across windows.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Pauli branches and current-syndrome recovery}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:correlated}]
By local finiteness, all events on the finite torus can
be ordered up to any finite deterministic horizon.
Starting in the code space, induction over them keeps
each branch a Pauli translate of its original encoded
input, with the reference untouched. Every stabilizer
predicate is a definite scalar on that branch and
extracts no logical information. A noise string
multiplies the Pauli frame regardless of its classical
correlations or adaptation. By the explicit
input-independence hypothesis, branch probabilities
do not depend on the encoded logical amplitudes.
Averaging these branches therefore defines the
storage channel on encoded inputs. This argument
does not assume an additive Lindblad generator
for the correlated noise or apply DOA's
Theorem 1 outside its Lindblad class.

Use exactly the decoder \(D\) of
Section~\ref{sec:decoder}. Lemma~\ref{lem:homology}
preserves the filled homology class across a single
face flip if every syndrome component on both
sides is small. For an atomic Pauli string,
apply the lemma successively to its single-face
factors in their prescribed internal order.
There is no intervening correction; each partial
product has the original time and fault cover.
Proposition~\ref{prop:ls-history} controls all these
intermediate syndromes, as well as the physical
ones. It would not suffice to inspect only the
two physical endpoints of an atomic error: a
whole logical membrane can have empty syndrome
at both endpoints. The specified order at a
clock/noise tie fixes the tested syndrome, and
the geometric implication covers that order.

Apply \eqref{eq:ls-history} to both marginal
sectors and use a union bound. With probability
at least \(1-2C_0(1+t)e^{-\beta L}\), all virtual
and physical stages through \(t\) have only small
components in both sectors. Their initial filled
classes are zero and remain zero across every
factor. After applying the two decoder fillings,
the residual Pauli is therefore a stabilizer up
to phase. This branch restores all six logical
qubits and any reference exactly.

The success event and its probability bound are
uniform over logical inputs and allowed Pauli
labels. For any pure \(|\Psi\rangle_{QR}\), the
target overlap is linear in the trajectory
mixture and nonnegative on every remaining
branch. Consequently
\[
 \langle\Psi|[(\readout\circ\mathsf{Store}_t\circ\enc)
              \otimes\id_R](|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|)
                 |\Psi\rangle
 \ge1-2C_0(1+t)e^{-\beta L}.
\]
Take \(c=\beta/3\) and enlarge \(C\), exactly as
in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, to obtain
\eqref{eq:correlated-memory} and its reference
version throughout \(0\le t\le e^{cL}\).
The decoder reads only the physical syndrome
at the requested time. Its virtual factorization
and auxiliary comparisons are proof devices,
not a history supplied to recovery.

The constants can be chosen without circularity:
fix the footprint geometry and \(A>100\), choose
\(b_*\) for the joint witness cost, enlarge \(B\)
for the clock-only term, then decrease \(p_*\)
for the fixed envelope size \(V_B\). Choose
\(L_0\) only after those choices. All are
independent of the admissible law and input.
\end{proof}
