\section{Additive finite-range Markovian Lindblad noise}\label{sec:general}

This section gives the strongest storage statement. The correction
generator is kept exact; the added noise can couple syndrome
coherences and need not preserve the identity. The stochastic-Pauli
theorems retain their separate conclusions, including the correlated
history model, which need not be Markovian.

\subsection{Noise strength and theorem}

Let
\[
 \mathcal A=\sum_p\bigl(\Diss[Z_p\Pi_p^X]+\Diss[X_p\Pi_p^Z]\bigr)
\]
be the unit-rate correction of \eqref{eq:generator}, without its Pauli
noise terms. Fix a microscopic interaction range \(r\), independently
of \(L\). Let \(\mathcal N\) be a time-independent Lindblad generator
whose semigroup is completely positive and trace preserving
(CPTP), with a decomposition into maps supported on qubit sets \(Y\)
of diameter at most \(r\), satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:incident-strength}
 \mathcal N=\sum_Y\mathcal N_Y,\qquad
 \sup_i\sum_{Y\ni i}\|\mathcal N_Y\|_\diamond\le\epsilon .
\end{equation}
The diamond norm is the completely bounded trace norm, including an
arbitrary untouched reference. The summands need not individually
generate channels; their sum must be a Lindblad generator.
An empty-support scalar term can be included in the
trace-preserving normalization.

A Hamiltonian contribution \(-i[H_Y,\cdot]\) has diamond norm at most
\(2\|H_Y\|\), and \(\Diss[J_Y]\) has norm at most \(2\|J_Y\|^2\):
these follow by bounding each left/right multiplication by its
operator norms, also after tensoring a reference. Thus
\[
 h\sum_i X_i\quad\text{has strength at most }2|h|,\qquad
 \gamma\sum_i\Diss[|0\rangle\langle1|_i]
       \quad\text{has strength at most }2\gamma .
\]
The coherent threshold applies to the amplitude \(|h|\), not \(h^2\).
Both non-unital dissipation, such as amplitude damping, and
bounded-range multi-qubit dissipation are allowed.

\begin{theorem}[General additive-noise storage]\label{thm:general}
For each fixed range \(r\) there exist
\(\epsilon_*(r),c,C>0\) and an integer \(L_0\) such that the following
holds uniformly for every integer \(L\ge L_0\) and every generator
\(\mathcal N\) satisfying \eqref{eq:incident-strength} with
\(\epsilon\le\epsilon_*(r)\).
Initialize the six logical qubits of the periodic four-dimensional
toric code by ideal encoding, evolve by
\(e^{t(\mathcal A+\mathcal N)}\), and apply the current-syndrome
decoder of Section~\ref{sec:decoder} in both sectors, followed by
inverse encoding. For every pure state \(|\Psi\rangle\) of all six
logical qubits and any untouched reference, and every deterministic
\(0\le t\le e^{cL}\), the decoded target overlap is at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-fidelity}
 1-Ce^{-cL}.
\end{equation}
The same worst-case bound holds for any designated logical qubit
with any fixed state of the unused qubits.
The constants and decoder are independent of the input,
reference dimension, noise generator within the bound, and read
time. Preparation and final measurement and decoding are ideal.
\end{theorem}

The noise is \emph{added} to the exact \(\mathcal A\).
Uniform changes of the correction rate are covered by time rescaling.
Small spatial rate variations are also covered by using a common
positive rate as baseline and treating the excess local correction
dissipators as additive noise of the corresponding incident strength.
No general assertion is made for replacing a correction jump
\(P\Pi\) by \(P\Pi+\delta K\): the difference of the two
dissipators need not itself be a Lindblad noise generator.
General non-Markovian quantum baths are outside this theorem.

\subsection{A local Pauli normal form and completely positive interpolation}

Use Hermitian physical Pauli strings \(P_a\), with \(P_0=I\).
For a dissipative coefficient indexed by \(a,b\), its
\emph{declared support} is
\(\supp(P_a)\cup\supp(P_b)\), even if a product \(P_bP_a\)
cancels some factors. Let \(v_r\) bound the number of qubits in a
range-\(r\) set.

\begin{lemma}[Local normal form]\label{lem:choi}
Every generator in \eqref{eq:incident-strength} has a representation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pauli-normal}
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal N(X)={}&-i\left[\sum_{a\ne0}h_aP_a,X\right]\\
 &+\sum_{a,b\ne0}c_{ab}
 \left(P_aXP_b-\tfrac12P_bP_aX-\tfrac12XP_bP_a\right),
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
where \(h_a\) are real and \(c\) is positive semidefinite.
Every nonzero coefficient has declared support in a range-\(r\)
set. For a constant \(C_r\), independent of \(L\), the sum of
absolute coefficients whose declared supports touch any specified
qubit is at most \(C_r\epsilon\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a local summand on \(Y\), use the normalized maximally
entangled vector \(|\Omega_Y\rangle\) and the orthonormal vectors
\(|P_a\rangle=(P_a\otimes I)|\Omega_Y\rangle\).
The entries \(d^Y_{ab}\) of its Choi matrix in this basis are exactly
its coefficients for \(X\mapsto P_aXP_b\).
Their moduli are at most \(\|\mathcal N_Y\|_\diamond\), because
the Choi trace norm is bounded by the diamond norm and bounds
each matrix element. There are at most \(16^{v_r}\) entries.
Tensoring identity maps outside \(Y\) only adds identity Pauli
factors. Sum these matrices in the global Pauli basis.

The principal nonidentity/nonidentity block \(c=(d_{ab})_{a,b\ne0}\)
is positive semidefinite. Indeed, compress the Choi matrix of
\(e^{s\mathcal N}\) orthogonally to \(|I\rangle\). It is positive,
vanishes at \(s=0\), and its derivative there is exactly \(c\);
division by \(s>0\) and the limit prove positivity.
Hermiticity preservation gives \(d=d^\dagger\).
Subtract the dissipative sum in \eqref{eq:pauli-normal}.
All its nonidentity/nonidentity entries cancel, leaving
\(GX+XG^\dagger\), with the real identity/identity entry
shared between the two legs. Both the original generator and
each dissipative bracket have zero trace on every input.
Hence \(G+G^\dagger=0\), so the remainder is a commutator
with a Hermitian operator.

Its coefficients arise only from identity-leg entries and products
\(P_bP_a\) belonging to the same local set \(Y\).
Drop a scalar Hamiltonian. The absolute coefficient bound,
including those products and cancellations, is therefore bounded
by a fixed multiple of \(16^{v_r}\) times the incident sum in
\eqref{eq:incident-strength}; for example \(C_r=5\cdot16^{v_r}\)
suffices. Products do not enlarge the declared local sets.
This proves the support and strength assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Label deletion and interpolation]\label{lem:label-interpolation}
Choose \(x_a\in[0,1]\), \(a\ne0\), and in \eqref{eq:pauli-normal}
replace \(h_a\) by \(x_ah_a\) and \(c_{ab}\) by \(x_ac_{ab}x_b\).
The resulting \(\mathcal N(x)\) generates CPTP evolution.
Deleting every label meeting a qubit region \(W\) makes that
evolution act as identity on \(W\).
For regions \(W_i\) separated pairwise by more than \(r\),
permanently delete any family of labels and scale each remaining
label meeting \(W_i\) by \(z_i\in[0,1]\), leaving the others at one.
Then
\[
 \partial_i\partial_j\mathcal N(z)=0\quad(i\ne j),
\]
and \(\partial_i\mathcal N(z)\) is a sum of Pauli sandwiches
\(X\mapsto uPXQ^\dagger\), with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:derivative-cost}
 \sum |u|\le4C_r\epsilon |W_i|
\end{equation}
uniformly in \(z\). Every such map acts trivially on all permanently
excluded regions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The new coefficient matrix is obtained from \(c\) by diagonal
congruence, and is positive semidefinite; the Hamiltonian remains
Hermitian. Thus the displayed normal form gives a Lindblad
generator. If all labels meeting \(W\) are zero, every surviving
operator has identity on \(W\), including products in the
anticommutators. The propagator consequently has Kraus operators
acting as identity there.

Tag a Hamiltonian monomial by \(a\), and every one of the three
summands of a dissipative monomial by both \(a,b\).
A substitution \(x_a=0\) deletes precisely the exponential-series
words using that label, including a tagged anticommutator product
proportional to identity. Since a declared support has diameter
at most \(r\), it cannot meet two separated \(W_i\).
Every surviving generator monomial therefore depends on at most
one \(z_i\), proving the mixed-derivative identity.
Its possible powers are \(z_i\) and \(z_i^2\), whose derivatives
on the unit interval have modulus at most two.
Expand the commutator and dissipative bracket into Pauli
sandwiches, putting all scalar phases in \(u\).
Their coefficient sums are bounded by
\eqref{eq:derivative-cost} using Lemma~\ref{lem:choi} and the
incident sum over \(W_i\).
Permanent deletions remain in force after differentiation.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Product approximation]\label{lem:product-general}
Partition each slab into slices of lengths \(d\le\delta\), with the
actual remaining duration in a final slice. In each slice apply
\(e^{d\mathcal N}\) and every local correction channel
\(e^{d\Diss[P\Pi]}\) in a fixed order.
For fixed \(L\) and finite \(t\), this product converges in diamond
norm to \(e^{t(\mathcal A+\mathcal N)}\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\).
A correction channel has the exact two-Kraus representation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:correction-kraus}
 K_0=I-\Pi+e^{-d/2}\Pi,\qquad
 K_1=\sqrt{1-e^{-d}}\,P\Pi .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The successful Pauli reverses both tested check signs, so
\(\Pi P\Pi=0\). On the four operator blocks associated with
\(\Pi\) and \(I-\Pi\), the active diagonal block loses a factor
\(e^{-d}\), the off-diagonal blocks gain \(e^{-d/2}\), and the
lost diagonal weight is transferred by \(P\).
The Kraus operators in \eqref{eq:correction-kraus} give exactly
these four solutions of the one-term master equation.
Their squared products sum to identity.

For a fixed finite system, a slice product and the full
exponential have the same linear term in \(d\). Their exponential
series remainders differ by at most \(O_L(d^2)\) for \(d\le1\).
All complete slice maps are CPTP and have diamond norm one.
Telescoping their products therefore gives total error
\(O_L(t\delta)\). Constants in this limiting argument may
depend on \(L\). The storage estimates below are uniform in
the mesh once it is sufficiently fine for the fixed block side.
No thermodynamic limit is interchanged with this product limit.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Intrinsic slab violations and protected regions}

Use the block geometry of Section~\ref{sec:local}, with
\(B\ge r+20\), enlarged for the fixed microscopic metric,
\(M=\lfloor L/B\rfloor\), \(b=L/M\), and \(T=1000B\).
The offsets \(\Delta,A_s\) are those of \eqref{eq:regions}.
For a full slab impose the following two implications in both
sectors: endpoint occupation of \(Q_z\) requires initial occupation
in every \(z+A_s\), and any visit to \(Q_z\) requires initial
occupation somewhere in \(U_z\). Visits include every intermediate
single-face factor of a noise Pauli. In a short terminal slab ending
at readout impose only the visit implication.

Let \(W_z\) contain the physical supports of all checks whose
syndrome edges have a vertex in either sector's \(U_z\), with a
fixed microscopic collar for the two native lattice conventions.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:protected-volume}
 |W_z|\le V_B=O_{R,r}(B^4).
\end{equation}
There is an integer \(D_{\rm pack}\), independent of \(B,L,t\),
such that from any \(m\) specified bins in one slab one can retain
at least \(m/D_{\rm pack}\) whose \(W_z\) are separated by more
than \(r\) and whose internal correction tapes are disjoint.
Because \(B\ge r+20\), each collar occupies only a fixed number of
coarse blocks and each retained region excludes a fixed coarse
neighbourhood, so \(D_{\rm pack}\) is independent of \(B\).
This is a greedy bounded-degree packing on the coarse lattice.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform internal service test]\label{lem:quantum-clock}
There is a test of the internal correction opportunities in a
protected box (no data-noise label meets \(W_z\), so interior edges change only by internal correction updates and by
boundary-face corrections toggling collar edges), for both sectors together, of failure probability
at most \(q_B\), with \(q_B\to0\) as \(B\to\infty\), such that
passing implies the two intrinsic implications for every allowed
initial check assignment and every boundary history.
For product slices the assertion holds for sufficiently small
mesh \(\delta\), uniformly over that boundary history.
It is used only when the internal tested check values are definite.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Run a clean cut on full faces in \(U_z\) with the restricted initial
edge assignment, coupled to the internal opportunities.
Exclude a chronological chain of \(m_f=1000T\) intersecting
opportunities ending within two cells of \(Q_z\).
The bound in Lemma~\ref{lem:bad-prob} is
\(CB^4(96eT/m_f)^{m_f}\).
A discrepancy from the boundary must travel through such an
internal suffix: initial interior edges agree, and the boundary
is at distance at least \(RB-2>m_f+5\).
The exclusion of other changes on inner edges concerns data-noise
changes, while a boundary-face correction may toggle an interior
collar edge and is included as boundary input in the discrepancy
trace.
Boundary evolution can otherwise be arbitrary.

For a failed coordinate predecessor condition, confinement keeps
the clean cut beyond the corresponding face of the target block.
For the fifth condition, its maximum initial height is at most
\(H=\lfloor b(\sum_i z_i+4)\rfloor\).
Parity is even in the interior northeast cones.
Lemma~\ref{lem:clearance} then clears each target vertex by its
two-pass schedule, with failure bound
\[
 (2B+1)^4(4\cdot2^{12})^{8B+2}e^{-T/2}.
\]
If \(U_z\) was initially empty, the clean cut stays empty.
These arguments give both implications for every initial
assignment. In a terminal interval of length less than \(T\),
only the fast-chain exclusion and visit implication are used,
and its probability is bounded by the full-\(T\) estimate.

For a product slice use one independent Bernoulli opportunity
per internal face with parameter \(1-e^{-d}\).
Each Bernoulli opportunity is the indicator of at least one mark
of that face's independent rate-one Poisson process in the slice,
and excluding slices with two or more marks across the entire
tested internal-face family makes every fixed serialization agree
with their chronological order.
If that family, including both sectors, has \(n_B=O_R(B^4)\)
faces, the second factorial moment bounds this collision
probability by \(n_B^2T\delta/2\).
Choose the mesh so this is at most \(e^{-B}\).
Twice the sum of the preceding two tails, plus \(e^{-B}\),
can be used as \(q_B\); it tends to zero.
Separate protected boxes have independent unconditioned tapes.
The next lemma justifies the use of definite internal predicates
despite a coherent exterior.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact linear histories and a one-leg filter}

For a product approximant expand each \(e^{d\mathcal N}\)
in its absolutely convergent series and each normal-form
coefficient into tagged Pauli sandwiches. Use a common
two-outcome correction record on ket and bra.
Each leg of a fine term is a Pauli word with scalar
no-jump factors on definite syndromes. Serialize all its
Pauli factors. For arbitrary input operators, resolve each
slab boundary by independent left and right global syndrome
projectors, retaining the off-diagonal blocks. These are
linear resolutions, not physical measurements.
For encoded input the ket and bra histories are already
syndrome definite whenever the corresponding amplitude is
nonzero. Absolute convergence at fixed finite \(L,t,\delta\)
justifies the regroupings below.

For a bin \(u=(z,k)\) set \(J_u=1\) if a tagged data label on
either leg meets \(W_z\), or, when there is no such label,
if the \emph{ket} violates an intrinsic implication in at
least one sector. Set \(\mathrm{good}_u=1-J_u\).
Zero-amplitude histories contribute zero.
In a protected box, remaining noise acts off all tracked
checks; a correction jump has a fixed Pauli shift of their
signs, even if its predicate reads other signs, and a no-jump
preserves them. The intrinsic mask is therefore determined
by initial tracked signs and the correction record.

Write \(\Phi_S\) for the linear map with exact bad-bin set
\(S\), before readout, and define its superset sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coherent-superset}
 \mathcal E(K)=\sum_{S\supseteq K}\Phi_S .
\end{equation}
Superset fault-path norms are standard in quantum fault-tolerance
analyses \cite{AGP,DST}; the estimate below is proved for the
present correction.
These maps need not be positive. Summing all \(\Phi_S\)
gives the physical product channel, with all independent
left and right syndrome resolutions included.

\begin{lemma}[Protected-halo filter]\label{lem:halo-filter}
In one slab permanently delete all data labels touching any
specified collection of protected regions. Between correction
gates allow exact interpolated exterior noise channels and
specified Pauli sandwiches acting off those regions, with
their scalar coefficients factored out.
For separated protected roots \(\mathcal F_0\), impose an
intrinsic violation on the ket at each root; on any further
protected roots one may impose both passing implications.
The resulting linear map has diamond norm at most
\(q_B^{|\mathcal F_0|/2}\), even when the bra is unrestricted
and its inserted Paulis differ from the ket's.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose Kraus records for every exterior channel and for each
correction gate. Let \(\Pi_s\) be the nonzero joint eigenspace
projectors of the union of tracked checks.
For a common record \(\omega\), its ket product \(K_\omega\)
maps \(\Pi_s\) into \(\Pi_{s\oplus d(\omega)}\):
a correction jump gives a fixed Pauli shift, each no-jump
commutes with all checks, and exterior factors act off their
supports. Boundary jumps may test unknown other checks
without changing this translation identity.
The intermediate tracked signs are fixed by \(s\) and the
jump prefix. Thus the ket acceptance mask \(m(s,\omega)\)
is a diagonal zero/one function of its initial check block.

With an orthonormal record register define
\[
 V_m=\sum_\omega|\omega\rangle\otimes
                 \sum_s m(s,\omega)K_\omega\Pi_s,\qquad
 V_R=\sum_\omega|\omega\rangle\otimes K^R_\omega .
\]
The unrestricted bra circuit is a composition of channels
and norm-one unitary insertions, so \(V_R\) is an isometry.
For fixed \(\omega\), the final translated check blocks
from different \(s\) are orthogonal. Hence
\[
 \|V_m\|^2=
 \max_s\sup_{\substack{\psi\in\operatorname{ran}\Pi_s\\\|\psi\|=1}}
       \sum_\omega m(s,\omega)\|K_\omega\psi\|^2 .
\]
There is no multiplicity factor for possible initial
check assignments. Additional passing masks only decrease
this nonnegative sum.

For fixed \(s,\psi\), sample the one-leg record distribution.
At an internal face the predicate stays definite after
every preceding outcome. An independent Bernoulli opportunity
of parameter \(1-e^{-d}\) gives exactly the correct
probabilities: accept on an opportunity when enabled,
and otherwise record no jump. The normalized vector is
unchanged or has the indicated Pauli applied.
Sample every other Kraus outcome from its ordinary
conditional probability given that vector.
Pre-sampling the independent internal tapes and using
fresh randomness elsewhere therefore reproduces the
one-leg distribution by the chain rule, also for states
entangled with the exterior.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:quantum-clock}, a violation in every
root of \(\mathcal F_0\) requires failure of all its
independent unconditioned tape tests, whatever boundary
records arise. The sum is at most \(q_B^{|\mathcal F_0|}\),
uniformly in \(\psi\) and references.
The filtered map is
\(X\mapsto\Tr_{\rm record}(V_m X V_R^\dagger)\).
Partial trace has diamond norm one, and this sandwich
has completely bounded trace norm at most
\(\|V_m\|\|V_R\|\).
Taking the square root proves the assertion.
The one-leg probability law was used only for this
squared-norm bound; no fictitious rejection record has
been attached to a physical no-jump amplitude.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Mixed differences and joint diamond cost}

\begin{proposition}[Superset bound with optional faults resummed]
\label{prop:coherent-joint}
For every \(b_*\in(0,1)\), one can choose \(B\), then a positive
\(\epsilon_*\), and then a mesh bound, all independently of
\(L,t\), so that for every admissible generator of strength
at most \(\epsilon_*\), every sufficiently large \(L\), every
finite read time, and every set \(K\) of real spacetime bins
in its product approximant,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coherent-joint}
 \|\mathcal E(K)\|_\diamond\le b_*^{|K|}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Begin with \(m\) required bins in one slab. Retain a separated
subfamily of size \(\ell\ge m/D_{\rm pack}\).
For every other required bin expand its indicator as
\(J_u=1-\mathrm{good}_u\), giving at most \(2^{m-\ell}\)
signed terms. A chosen good indicator permanently deletes
all labels meeting its \(W_z\) and imposes both ket passing
implications. Delete the union of such labels when regions
overlap; keep all passing masks. This accounts for every
unselected required restriction.

At a retained root its bad indicator has two disjoint
alternatives: a tagged label touches its region, or no
label touches it and its ket has an intrinsic violation.
Partition the retained roots into \(\mathcal I\) of the
former kind and \(\mathcal F_0\) of the latter.
Permanently delete all labels touching the clean regions,
including \(\mathcal F_0\), and impose their masks and the
previous passing masks. No intrinsic mask is imposed at
\(\mathcal I\).
Scale every remaining label meeting \(W_i\), \(i\in\mathcal I\),
by \(z_i\) as in Lemma~\ref{lem:label-interpolation}.
The product of differences between \(z_i=1\) and \(z_i=0\)
selects exactly the words having a tagged appearance at every
such root: a missing variable cancels, while any positive
power contributes \(1^a-0^a=1\).
Both-leg tags on all anticommutator terms are retained.

This difference can be evaluated with the masks fixed:
on permanently protected regions their initial values and
correction record determine them, independently of exterior
Kraus records and of the remaining noise-word expansion.
Equivalently fix those correction records and input syndrome
projectors while taking the difference and sum them afterwards.

Represent the mixed difference by the integral of the mixed
derivative over \([0,1]^{|\mathcal I|}\).
For a noise interval the exact Duhamel identity is
\[
 \partial_i e^{d\mathcal N(z)}
 =\int_0^d e^{(d-s)\mathcal N(z)}
       (\partial_i\mathcal N(z))e^{s\mathcal N(z)}\,\dd s .
\]
It follows by differentiating the exponential series or by
variation of constants. Iterating distinct derivatives inserts
each \(\partial_i\mathcal N\) exactly once, because
\(\partial_i\partial_j\mathcal N=0\) for separated roots.
All chronological placements are included, even within a
single noise interval. For labeled insertion times their
ordering simplices partition the product of
\(|\mathcal I|\) copies of the union of noise intervals.
That union has total duration at most \(T\) per variable,
so the integration measure is at most \(T^{|\mathcal I|}\).
The interpolation cube has volume one; correction gates
between intervals use no noise-integration time.

Expand each designated derivative into Pauli sandwiches.
By \eqref{eq:derivative-cost} their absolute coefficient
sum is at most \(4C_rV_B\epsilon\).
Every remaining exact propagator is CPTP and acts off all
permanently protected regions. The designated sandwiches
also act off them, even when an extra passing region overlaps
a dirty root, because its touching labels were deleted.
For fixed insertion times and labels apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:halo-filter}. The resulting partition norm is
at most
\[
 (4C_rTV_B\epsilon)^{|\mathcal I|}
                       q_B^{|\mathcal F_0|/2}.
\]
All optional fault words have already been resummed into
CPTP propagators before norms are taken.
There is no factor exponential in their spacetime volume.

Sum the partitions and earlier signs. The one-slab norm
is at most
\[
 2^m(4C_rTV_B\epsilon+\sqrt{q_B})^\ell .
\]
Choose \(B\) large enough that
\(\sqrt{q_B}\le(b_*/2)^{D_{\rm pack}}/2\), then
\(\epsilon_*>0\) so that
\(4C_rTV_B\epsilon_*\le(b_*/2)^{D_{\rm pack}}/2\).
Choose the mesh small enough for the clock bound.
Since \(D_{\rm pack}\ell\ge m\), this gives a bound
\(2^m(b_*/2)^{D_{\rm pack}\ell}\le b_*^m\).

For several slabs, the restricted maps compose in time
order. Their independent left and right boundary syndrome
resolutions are exactly those required in the following
slab; their sum preserves all cross blocks.
No record is shared between slabs except through the data
operator. A slab with no requirement gives its original
CPTP product. Diamond-norm submultiplicativity therefore
gives \eqref{eq:coherent-joint}. The short terminal slab
uses only visit masks and the same upper bounds.
The order of choices leaves a strictly positive strength
threshold independent of \(L,t\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Walk extraction on the finite torus}

Use the product metric \(\rho\) of \eqref{eq:productmetric}.
A unit-step walk has successive sites at distance at most one.

\begin{lemma}[A crossing yields a short walk through many distinct marks]
\label{lem:coherent-walk}
There are positive coarse constants \(a,C_0\), independent of
\(B,L,t\), such that a fine ket history from the code with a
syndrome component of diameter at least \(L/100\), in either
sector at any physical or serialized stage, supplies \(k\ge aM\)
distinct real bins with \(J_u=1\) and a unit-step walk of length
at most \(C_0k\) visiting all those bins.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For the chosen sector define the all-one comparisons and visit
proxies by \eqref{eq:comparison}--\eqref{eq:proxy}, using the
intrinsic marks \(J\).
A good full bin has the five endpoint implications, so physical
boundary occupation forces a comparison zero by induction.
Every visit forces the corresponding proxy, including virtual
noise stages. The partial final slab uses only this visit
implication; its artificial endpoint bit need not describe
the readout state.

Take \(h=\lfloor M/[100(R+6)]\rfloor\), and initialize a comparison
\(H_j\) at each \(a_j=jh\). Extend \(J\) by zero beyond the last
real slab up to the final needed overlap.
Require equality of \(H_j,H_{j+1}\) at \(a_{j+2}\), and absence
of a spatially connected proxy set of diameter at least \(M/200\)
at every date in that two-epoch window.
If every test passes, comparison equality propagates across the
seams exactly as in Lemma~\ref{lem:iteration}: at the beginning
of epoch one it is identity, common maps propagate it, and the
next overlap equality changes the recent comparison.
Predecessor proxies agree on the same dates.
A large physical or virtual component would give visited block
diameter at least \(M/100-1\ge M/200\), a contradiction.

Thus some deterministic window test fails.
For an endpoint disagreement the older bit is zero and the newer
one is one. Trace disagreeing predecessors back to the newer
initialization date. This gives a path of older zeros, hence
proxy sites, with temporal span \(h\) and steps at most \(R\).
For a spatial test failure choose a connected spatial path
reaching diameter \(M/200\).
In either case the endpoints of a proxy path in a depth-\(2h\)
window have distance
\[
 s\ge\min\{h,M/200\}.
\]
Only real marks can serve as sinks; the artificially appended
future has none.

For each proxy site, a direct mark is a one-sink certificate.
Otherwise take its zero predecessor and its finite backward
cone down to the all-one window bottom.
Every time section lifts injectively, since \(4Rh<M/2\).
Copy good maps, put constant-zero maps at marks and constant-one
maps at the bottom and outside.
The typed graph is finite, acyclic, and has nonempty predecessor
sets \(A_s\) at every nonconstant node. Its root value is the
maximal trajectory value.
SST Theorem 22 and Definitions 17 and 21, with Eq.~(2.16),
give a connected five-charge contour with distinct bad sinks,
avoiding the constant-one cutoff \cite{SST}.
The sink images remain distinct physical bins by the
time-section injectivity.
With \(n\) edges per charge and \(m\) sinks, the directly
checked forms \eqref{eq:polar} give
\(n\le(1+8R)(m-1)\), as in \eqref{eq:edges-sinks}
and SST Lemmas 38 and 40.
Thus no stochastic-independence assumption enters this step.

The contour and the link to its proxy root have unit-step
connecting length \(O_R(n+1)\), and all sinks lie in the
closed ball of radius \(r_i=10(R+6)(n+1)\) about that root.
This includes the bounded predecessor link when \(n=0\).
Choose one ball per proxy-path site, and retain disjoint
balls greedily in decreasing radius.
Their triple enlargements cover the original path.
A simple chain of retained centers connects its endpoint
covers, with successive distances at most
\(3r_i+3r_{i+1}+R\).
As in Proposition~\ref{prop:path},
\(s\le7\sum_i r_i\).

Each ball supplies at least
\((n+1)/(2+8R)\) distinct sinks. Different retained balls
have disjoint physical sink sets, even when balls wrap.
Writing \(k\) for their total number, it follows that
\(\sum_i r_i\le C_Rk\), and hence \(k\ge aM\) for a
positive fixed \(a\) at sufficiently large \(M\).
Join successive centers by shortest unit-step paths and
attach each contour's connecting graph.
Its total number of edges is \(O_R(k)\).
A spanning-tree tour traverses each edge at most twice
and visits every distinct sink in at most \(C_0k\) steps.
The walk may pass through unmarked bins; it is used to
locate its distinct marked keys.
All constants refer only to the fixed coarse geometry.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Dense subsets and the superset polynomial}

Decrease \(a\) if necessary so \(a\le1\), and let \(S_0\) be the
largest power of two not exceeding \(aM/2\).
For large \(M\),
\[
 aM/4\le S_0\le aM/2.
\]
For dyadic integers \(1\le r'\le S_0\) put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density-function}
 g(r')=\gamma\left(\sqrt{r'}+\frac{(r')^2}{S_0}\right),
\end{equation}
where the positive coarse constant \(\gamma\) is chosen below.
Call a nonempty set \(W\) of bin keys dense if every \(u\in W\)
has at least \(g(r')\) members of \(W\) in its
\(\rho\)-ball of radius \(r'\), at every such dyadic scale.

\begin{lemma}[Extraction at a linear top scale]\label{lem:dense-extraction}
The marked keys of a crossing in Lemma~\ref{lem:coherent-walk}
contain a dense subset, for a fixed \(\gamma>0\) independent
of \(B,L,t\). Every dense set has at least \(\gamma S_0\)
members.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Place the \(k\) distinct keys at one chosen occurrence of each
on its walk. Pad the list with empty slots to a power-of-two
length \(n\), so \(k\le n\le C_1k\) with fixed \(C_1\).
At the dyadic scales \(r'=1,2,\ldots,S_0\), in increasing order,
discard every currently selected key in each aligned slot
interval of length \(r'\) having fewer than \(g(r')\) keys.
The number discarded at that scale is at most
\((n/r')g(r')\), and
\[
 \sum_{\substack{r'\le S_0\\r'\ {\rm dyadic}}}
                 \frac{g(r')}{r'}
 \le\gamma\left(\frac{1}{1-2^{-1/2}}+2\right).
\]
Choose \(\gamma\) small enough that \(C_1\) times this bound
is at most \(1/2\).
At least \(k/2\) keys survive.

For a surviving key its \(r'\)-interval retained at least
\(g(r')\) keys at that pass. Every subsequent larger dyadic
interval either misses this interval or contains it.
Deleting a containing parent would delete the surviving key
as well, so none of those retained keys can later be removed.
Walk times in the interval differ by less than \(r'\),
so their actual key locations have distance at most \(r'\).
Padding added no keys. The final set is therefore dense.
At the top scale a dense set has at least
\(g(S_0)\ge\gamma S_0\) members, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

Let \(\mathcal U\) be the \(M^4\) spatial blocks times the real
slabs up to readout, and \(N_w=|\mathcal U|\ge1\).
Define \(f(S)=1\) when \(S\subseteq\mathcal U\) contains
some dense subset, and zero otherwise. This enlarged
indicator may also select correctable histories.

\begin{lemma}[Superset polynomial and dense-set count]
\label{lem:dense-count}
For
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mobius}
 a_K=\sum_{S\subseteq K}(-1)^{|K|-|S|}f(S)
\end{equation}
one has \(f(S)=\sum_{K\subseteq S}a_K\),
\(a_\varnothing=0\), and \(|a_K|\le2^{|K|}\).
Every \(K\) with nonzero coefficient is dense.
The number of dense sets of size \(m\) is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dense-count}
 \exp\left(C_2m+C_3\frac{m}{S_0}\log(2N_w)\right),
\end{equation}
where \(C_2,C_3\) depend only on coarse geometry.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Interchanging the finite subset sums proves inversion:
the coefficient of \(f(T)\) in \(\sum_{K\subseteq S}a_K\)
is \(\sum_{T\subseteq K\subseteq S}(-1)^{|K|-|T|}\),
which is one for \(T=S\) and zero otherwise.
The elementary bounds and empty coefficient follow.

If a point \(u\in K\) belongs to no dense subset of \(K\),
then adding or deleting \(u\) from any subset has no
effect on \(f\). Pair those two subsets in
\eqref{eq:mobius}; their contributions cancel.
Thus a nonzero coefficient requires every point of \(K\)
to belong to a dense subset of \(K\).
Their union, equal to \(K\), is dense: at each point
one of its dense subsets supplies all the required
neighbours. This proves the support assertion.

For counting, cut the circular spatial coordinates at the
fixed origin, and use aligned dyadic cubes in four spatial
coordinates and one time coordinate, clipped at domain ends.
In each occupied side-\(r'\) cube select a representative
in \(K\). Its radius-\(r'\) ball contains at least \(g(r')\)
keys. A fixed key lies in such balls from at most a fixed
number of cubes, since a radius-\(r'\) coordinate interval
meets only boundedly many aligned side-\(r'\) intervals,
also across the periodic seam. Double counting shows that
the number of occupied cubes at this scale is at most
\(C m/g(r')\).

At top scale \(S_0\) at most \(N_w\) cubes can be occupied.
Choosing up to \(Cm/g(S_0)\) roots costs at most
\((2N_w)^{C'm/S_0}\), enlarging the constant for rounding;
a nonempty dense set already has \(m\ge\gamma S_0\).
For each occupied nonunit cube specify the subset of its
32 children that are occupied. These masks specify the
unit keys exactly.
The total number of such decisions is \(O(m)\), since
\[
 \sum_{\substack{r'\le S_0\\r'\ {\rm dyadic}}}
        \frac{1}{g(r')}
 \le\gamma^{-1}\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j/2}<\infty .
\]
Their choices therefore cost \(e^{C_2m}\).
Combining with the root choices gives
\eqref{eq:dense-count}.
This counts arbitrary dense unions, including unions
of overlapping witnesses from different epochs.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Summed bad map without a volume-time loss]
\label{prop:general-sum}
There are fixed \(b_*,B,\epsilon_*>0\), chosen in that order,
such that for \(0<t\le e^M\) and sufficiently fine mesh,
the product-channel maps satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-bad-map}
 \left\|\sum_S f(S)\Phi_S\right\|_\diamond
 \le 2e^{-\gamma S_0\log2}.
\end{equation}
The constants are uniform in the admissible generator and read time.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The subset inversion and definition \eqref{eq:coherent-superset}
give the exact finite identity
\[
 \sum_S f(S)\Phi_S=\sum_K a_K\mathcal E(K).
\]
For \(0<t\le e^M\),
\(N_w=M^4\lceil t/T\rceil\) has
\(\log(2N_w)\le C_4M\) for all large \(M\), with \(C_4\)
independent of \(B\), since \(T\ge1\).
Together with \(S_0\ge aM/4\),
Lemma~\ref{lem:dense-count} bounds the number of nonzero
coefficient supports of size \(m\) by \(e^{C_5m}\), with
\(C_5\) independent of \(B\).
Choose \(b_*>0\) so \(2b_*e^{C_5}\le1/2\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:coherent-joint} then chooses \(B\),
the noise strength threshold, and the mesh bound.
Only dense supports occur and \(m\ge\lceil\gamma S_0\rceil\),
so the triangle inequality gives
\[
 \left\|\sum_K a_K\mathcal E(K)\right\|_\diamond
 \le\sum_{m\ge\lceil\gamma S_0\rceil}
                    (2b_*e^{C_5})^m
 \le2e^{-\gamma S_0\log2}.
\]
No unmeasured support indicator has been assigned a physical
probability, and no factor proportional to the number of
possible locations or observation dates was introduced.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Linear readout and passage to the exact semigroup}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:general}]
Use the fixed decoder of Section~\ref{sec:decoder}.
Its small-component branch fills every cycle inside its
embedded coordinate box. Across a single-face flip,
Lemma~\ref{lem:homology} makes
\(p+D(s)+D(s+\partial p)\) a boundary whenever both
syndromes have only components of diameter less than \(L/100\).
This holds for all merges and splits and in both sectors.

Consider a fine ket history whose exact bad set \(S\)
has \(f(S)=0\).
The contrapositive of Lemmas~\ref{lem:coherent-walk}
and \ref{lem:dense-extraction} excludes a large
component at every stage in either sector.
Serialize each inserted Pauli on the ket into its
single-face factors. Every intermediate stage was
included in the intrinsic visit test.
Correction jumps are themselves single-face flips;
no-jumps multiply the definite syndrome leg by a scalar.
Thus both filled homology classes stay zero from
the encoded start. After applying the decoder the
ket is the original logical ket, including its
reference, up to its scalar. Phases are absorbed
into that scalar.

This implication is applied to a linear cross term,
not to a classical mixture. At code input the
left and right syndrome projectors evaluate on
their respective Pauli-translated legs.
In the final measurement branch of the ket's
syndrome, a different bra syndrome vanishes.
If the bra syndrome matches, that bra can be
arbitrary, but the decoded ket is still in the
original logical ray.
For the pure target \(|\Psi\rangle\), put
\(Q_\Psi=I-|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|\) on the
logical output and reference.
Every such decoded cross term has zero expectation
against \(Q_\Psi\), because \(Q_\Psi\) annihilates
its ket. No bra diagonality or independence is
required.

The full product channel is trace preserving, so
its decoded infidelity is its expectation of
\(Q_\Psi\). The preceding exact vanishing gives
the equality of linear functionals
\[
 1-F_\Psi=
 \Tr\left[Q_\Psi\,
  (\mathcal R^{(6)}\otimes\id)
  \left(\sum_S f(S)\Phi_S\otimes\id\right)
  (\rho_{\Psi,{\rm enc}})\right],
\]
where \(\mathcal R^{(6)}\) is the physical
syndrome decoder returning all six logical qubits
and \(\rho_{\Psi,{\rm enc}}\) is the encoded
pure input. Its absolute value is at most the
diamond norm in \eqref{eq:general-bad-map}:
the input has trace norm one, the readout is
CPTP, and \(\|Q_\Psi\|\le1\).
Take this equality before the norm; all coherent
cross terms and the unrestricted bra normalization
have already been retained.

Consequently, for \(0<t\le e^M\),
\[
 1-F_\Psi\le2e^{-\beta M},\qquad
                  \beta=\gamma a\log2/4 .
\]
For each fixed \(L,t\), send the mesh to zero.
Lemma~\ref{lem:product-general} and continuity of
the readout and expectation give the same bound
for the exact \(e^{t(\mathcal A+\mathcal N)}\).
This takes a finite-system product limit, with
all preceding estimates uniform in its mesh.

Once \(B\) is fixed, \(M\ge L/(2B)\) for large \(L\).
Choose \(c\le\min\{1,\beta\}/(2B)\).
Then \(t\le e^{cL}\) implies \(t\le e^M\), and
\(2e^{-\beta M}\le2e^{-cL}\).
At zero recovery is exact; enlarging \(C\)
gives the stated uniform bound.
The choices were: coarse walk and density
constants, then \(b_*\), then \(B\), then
\(\epsilon_*(r)\), then mesh, with \(L_0\)
chosen after the fixed geometric parameters.
There is no dependence on the logical input
or reference dimension.

For a designated qubit and fixed unused logical
state, purify that state into an additional
untouched reference and apply the all-six-qubit
bound. After discarding unused outputs,
the projector onto the designated pure target
contains the full target projector, so its overlap
cannot decrease.
This proves the designated-qubit and reference
claims with the same syndrome-only decoder.
\end{proof}
