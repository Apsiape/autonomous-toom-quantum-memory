\title{An autonomous four-dimensional quantum memory\\
from a time-independent Toom Lindbladian}
\author{Seth Douglas \and Nidhal Mghirbi}
\date{November 22, 2026}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
We prove exponential storage for the time-independent four-dimensional
Toom dissipative toric code under any sufficiently weak additive
finite-range Markovian Lindblad noise generator, including coherent
Hamiltonian terms and non-unital dissipation such as amplitude damping.
From an encoded start, a decoder reading only the current syndrome
has worst-case fidelity at least \(1-Ce^{-cL}\) at every
\(0\le t\le e^{cL}\), in both CSS sectors and for all six logical
qubits entangled with an untouched reference.
The coherent threshold applies to the amplitude \(|h|\), not \(h^2\).
For such additive noise this realizes the fully autonomous memory
setting of Drechsler, Oreg and Aharonov, with translation invariance
when the noise is homogeneous.
The correction generator is exact; perturbations of its own jump
operators and general non-Markovian baths remain open.
Separate stochastic-Pauli theorems give explicit syndrome structure
and allow spacetime local stochastic bounded-range multi-qubit and
adaptive faults on unit time cells (one inverse correction rate),
with a noise law independent of the logical input.
On \(\Z^4\), the independent-noise syndrome process has exponential
component-diameter tails uniformly in time from empty syndrome.
The corner convention is related to Pastawski, Clemente and Cirac's
rule by \(x\mapsto-x\) and a uniform \(X/Z\) relabelling.
The general proof combines Poisson clearance and Toom contours with
a coherent protected-region estimate, exact mixed differences and
dense-set counting. The thresholds are non-explicit and
astronomically small, and the size requirements are astronomically
large.
\end{abstract}
